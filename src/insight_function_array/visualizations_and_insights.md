| Visualization                   | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Insights an analyst intends to notice—whether present or absent                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bar or column chart**         | Discrete categories represented by bars whose lengths or heights encode a measure. It is primarily used to compare magnitude across categories. ([Tableau Help][1])                                                                                                       | • Which category is largest or smallest?<br>• Is there a clear ranking?<br>• Does one category dominate?<br>• Is there a large gap between first and second place?<br>• Are any categories unusually high or low?<br>• Is there a long tail of small categories?<br>• Are the categories nearly equal, with no meaningful leader?<br>• Are positive and negative values mixed?<br>• Are expected categories absent or zero?                                                                                                           |
| **Line graph**                  | Points ordered along a continuous axis—usually time—and connected to emphasize the shape and progression of values. ([Microsoft Learn][2])                                                                                                                                | • Is there an upward, downward, or flat trend?<br>• Is the rate of change accelerating or decelerating?<br>• Are there spikes or sudden drops?<br>• Is there a persistent step change or inflection point?<br>• Are there cycles or seasonal patterns?<br>• Has volatility increased or decreased?<br>• Has the series entered a plateau?<br>• Do multiple lines cross, converge, or diverge?<br>• Are expected cycles, trends, or reactions absent?<br>• Are dates or periods missing?                                               |
| **Area or stacked-area chart**  | A line chart with the area beneath the line filled. Stacked forms show how components contribute to a changing total over time. ([Tableau Help][3])                                                                                                                       | • Is the total growing, shrinking, or stable?<br>• Which category contributes most to the total?<br>• Is one category gaining share while another loses share?<br>• Does a new category emerge or an existing one disappear?<br>• Is the composition stable over time?<br>• Are changes caused by the whole population or one component?<br>• Is apparent growth merely cumulative rather than a change in rate?<br>• Is a category expected to contribute but visibly absent?                                                        |
| **Scatter or bubble plot**      | Observations positioned by two numeric measures. Bubble size or color may encode additional measures or categories. It is used to examine relationships and distribution in two or more dimensions. ([Tableau Help][1])                                                   | • Is there a positive, negative, or no apparent correlation?<br>• Is the relationship linear or curved?<br>• Are there distinct clusters?<br>• Are groups clearly separated or heavily overlapping?<br>• Are there isolated outliers?<br>• Does variability increase as one measure increases?<br>• Are there empty regions or unexplained gaps?<br>• Does the relationship reverse for a subgroup?<br>• Is an expected relationship conspicuously absent?                                                                            |
| **Pie or donut chart**          | A circle divided into slices representing each category’s proportion of a whole. It works best with a relatively small number of categories. ([Tableau][4])                                                                                                               | • Does one slice dominate?<br>• Is the whole concentrated in a few categories?<br>• Are the shares nearly equal?<br>• Is the composition fragmented among many small slices?<br>• Is there a long tail that should be grouped as “Other”?<br>• Has one category crossed an important share threshold?<br>• Is an expected category absent?<br>• Do the slices actually sum to a meaningful whole?                                                                                                                                     |
| **Treemap**                     | Hierarchical categories shown as nested rectangles. Rectangle size usually represents magnitude, while color can represent a second measure. ([Tableau Help][5])                                                                                                          | • Which hierarchy branch dominates?<br>• Is value concentrated in a few leaves or branches?<br>• Are there many small categories forming a long tail?<br>• Which subcategories are unusually large or small within their parent?<br>• Does a large rectangle have an unexpectedly poor color-coded result?<br>• Are parts of the hierarchy sparse or missing?<br>• Does the hierarchy contain an unexpected concentration?<br>• Are child proportions materially different across parent groups?                                      |
| **Geographic map**              | Values encoded by geographic location using symbols, filled regions, density, paths, or flows. Maps are intended to answer spatial questions. ([Tableau Help][6])                                                                                                         | • Where are hotspots and cold spots?<br>• Are values geographically clustered?<br>• Are neighboring areas behaving similarly or differently?<br>• Are there regional outliers?<br>• Is there a geographic gradient or boundary effect?<br>• Are problems concentrated along a route or corridor?<br>• Are there flows between origins and destinations?<br>• Are expected regions missing or unrepresented?<br>• Does proximity appear related to the outcome?<br>• Would a non-map visualization reveal the difference more clearly? |
| **Heat map or highlight table** | A grid whose cell color represents a measure across combinations of row and column categories. A Tableau highlight table emphasizes comparison through color; Power BI tables and matrices can use conditional formatting to produce similar effects. ([Tableau Help][7]) | • Which cells are hotspots or cold spots?<br>• Is an entire row or column unusually high or low?<br>• Are there diagonal, block, or banded patterns?<br>• Is one combination of dimensions exceptional?<br>• Are positive and negative regions clearly separated?<br>• Is there a cluster of related problem cells?<br>• Are expected combinations blank or absent?<br>• Is the pattern consistent across groups or isolated?<br>• Does color reveal something that individual numbers obscure?                                       |
| **Table or matrix**             | Exact values arranged in rows and columns. Matrix visuals add hierarchical grouping, subtotals, aggregation, and drill-down. ([Microsoft Learn][2])                                                                                                                       | • What are the exact values?<br>• Which values exceed or fall below a threshold?<br>• Are totals and subtotals internally consistent?<br>• Are there missing, null, or zero values?<br>• Has rank changed between periods?<br>• Are signs reversed or values unexpectedly negative?<br>• Are repeated values suspiciously identical?<br>• Are there exceptional cells hidden among many ordinary ones—the “Where’s Waldo?” problem?<br>• Is an expected row, member, or period absent?                                                |
| **Histogram**                   | A numeric measure divided into ranges, or bins, with bar height representing the number of observations in each range. It shows the shape of a distribution. ([Tableau Help][8])                                                                                          | • Where is the center of the distribution?<br>• How wide is the spread?<br>• Is the distribution symmetric or skewed?<br>• Is there one peak or several peaks?<br>• Are there unusually heavy tails?<br>• Are there extreme outliers?<br>• Are there empty gaps between groups of values?<br>• Is there an unusual concentration at zero?<br>• Does a supposedly continuous measure appear discrete?<br>• Is the expected distribution shape absent?                                                                                  |
| **Box-and-whisker plot**        | A standardized distribution summary showing median, quartiles, spread, whiskers, and possible outliers. It is particularly useful for comparing distributions across groups. ([Tableau Help][9])                                                                          | • Which group has the highest or lowest median?<br>• Do groups have materially different spreads?<br>• Are there outliers within particular groups?<br>• Is the distribution skewed?<br>• Do the groups overlap substantially?<br>• Is one group more variable than the others?<br>• Is the difference in medians meaningful or negligible?<br>• Are expected group differences absent?<br>• Did the distribution change even though the average did not?                                                                             |
| **Combination chart**           | Multiple mark types—commonly bars and a line—placed in one view to compare related measures, sometimes with separate axes. ([Tableau Help][10])                                                                                                                           | • Do the two measures rise and fall together?<br>• Does one measure lead or lag the other?<br>• Does volume change while rate remains stable?<br>• Does actual performance track a target or benchmark?<br>• Are the measures diverging or converging?<br>• Is a change in one measure followed by a change in the other?<br>• Is the apparent relationship merely caused by incompatible scales?<br>• Is an expected response in the second measure absent?                                                                          |
| **Waterfall chart**             | A running total showing how successive positive and negative contributions transform a starting value into an ending value. ([Microsoft Learn][2])                                                                                                                        | • Which factors contributed most positively or negatively?<br>• What caused the change from beginning to ending value?<br>• Do gains offset losses?<br>• Is one contribution disproportionately important?<br>• Is there an unexplained residual?<br>• Does the sequence reconcile correctly to the ending total?<br>• Are expected costs, gains, or adjustments absent?<br>• Is deterioration caused by many small effects or one large effect?                                                                                      |
| **Funnel chart**                | Sequential stages whose widths represent the number or value remaining as items move through a process such as acquisition, qualification, purchase, or resolution. ([Microsoft Learn][2])                                                                                | • Where is the largest stage-to-stage drop?<br>• What is the conversion rate between stages?<br>• Is there a bottleneck?<br>• Is leakage concentrated at one stage or distributed throughout?<br>• Does a stage unexpectedly gain items?<br>• Is an expected stage missing?<br>• Do different segments have different drop-off patterns?<br>• Has conversion changed over time?<br>• Is the apparent problem before or after the current team’s area of responsibility?                                                               |
| **KPI, card, or gauge**         | A compact visual emphasizing one measure, often with a target, status, variance, or trend indicator. ([Microsoft Learn][2])                                                                                                                                               | • Is the current value above or below target?<br>• How large is the variance from target?<br>• Is status good, marginal, or critical?<br>• Is the measure improving or deteriorating?<br>• Has a threshold been crossed?<br>• Is performance stable but still unacceptable?<br>• Is there no data or stale data rather than a true zero?<br>• Is a favorable headline number hiding deterioration in its components?                                                                                                                  |
