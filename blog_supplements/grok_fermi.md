### What inspired the approach
This is a classic Fermi-style estimation problem (like “How many piano tuners are there in Chicago?”). Direct data on “people making inari in Boise today” does not exist, so I broke the question into population, demographic, behavioral, and commercial pieces that *can* be estimated or looked up, then combined them with rough frequency assumptions.

### Work / steps
- **Population base**: Greater Boise / Treasure Valley / Boise metro ≈ 864,000–876,000 (2025 Census Bureau estimates and 2026 COMPASS local estimates). Used ~870,000.  
- **Households**: Average household size in the area is roughly 2.5–2.7 → ≈ 320,000–350,000 households.  
- **Relevant demographics**:  
  - Asian population in the Boise metro ≈ 30,600.  
  - Japanese-descent population: Ada County roughly 4,600–5,200, Canyon County ~750 → metro total on the order of 5,500–6,000 people (~2,200–2,400 households).  
- **Local context**: There are roughly 20 sushi restaurants/bars in the Boise area (some list inari on kids’ menus or as a simple item). Multiple Asian markets (Idaho Capital Asian Market, Asia Market, Orient Market, etc.) stock Japanese ingredients, including seasoned fried tofu pouches (inari age / aburaage).  
- **Behavioral context**: Inari is one of the easiest sushi styles to make at home (buy the pouches, season rice, stuff—no rolling skill required). It is popular with kids, beginners, and as a vegan/vegetarian option. Broader surveys on home sushi-making show that a modest fraction of people have tried it at least once (figures in the 15–25% range appear in some country surveys), but regular home makers are a much smaller niche outside major Japanese or Asian-American communities.  

**Frequency-based breakdown (rough order-of-magnitude)**:  
- Japanese-descent households: Assume ~20% prepare inari a few times a year (say average 4 times/year). That yields on the order of ~5 household preparations per day.  
- Broader Asian households + serious Japanese-food enthusiasts: Larger pool. Added a contribution on the order of 10–20 people/households per day.  
- General population home cooks who occasionally make Japanese items: Small percentage of the ~330k households. Contributes only a few more on a typical day.  
- Restaurant/commercial prep: Of the ~20 sushi places, a subset prepare inari on any given day. This adds roughly 10 people (chefs/prep staff) on a typical weekday.  

Adding these pieces lands in the 30–50 range for a normal Tuesday in early November (no special Japanese holiday or major local event). Most of the activity is home-based rather than commercial.

The estimate treats “people who make” as individuals (or the primary cook in a household) actively preparing it that day. It does not count every diner or every restaurant customer. On a quiet Tuesday the number is probably toward the lower end of the range; it would spike around holidays, potlucks, or school events.
