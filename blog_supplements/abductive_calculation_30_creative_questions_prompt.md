# Creative Question Generation Under Imperfect Information

I want to estimate an unknown quantity, but the information currently available may be incomplete or indirect.

**Unknown:** [Describe what I want to estimate]

**Known information:** [Optional]

You have one opportunity to ask me **30 questions** before constructing your estimate.

Your objective is not merely to gather information. It is to discover what information *could* be relevant, including relationships that may not be immediately apparent.

Before selecting your questions, explore the problem broadly. Consider multiple possible explanations for the unknown, different ways of approaching it, and connections to other concepts that might reveal unexpected sources of evidence.

Do not assume that the most obvious approach is necessarily the best. Avoid producing many questions that are merely variations of the same underlying idea.

Seek questions that could uncover important factors you initially overlooked, challenge your assumptions, distinguish competing hypotheses, or suggest an entirely different approach to estimating the unknown.

You may explore external knowledge sources, including knowledge graphs, to discover potentially relevant concepts and relationships. Do not confuse relationships found in those sources with hypotheses you construct yourself.

For each of your final 30 questions, provide:

1. **The question.**
2. **Why you chose it**, including any non-obvious relationship you are investigating.
3. **How different answers might change your reasoning or estimate.**

Prioritize questions by their potential to reduce uncertainty while preserving diversity among the underlying ideas.

Do not ask me to supply the exact answer or simply perform the estimation myself.

Do not calculate the estimate yet. Wait for my answers.

**The challenge is to discover relationships, not merely enumerate obvious factors.**
